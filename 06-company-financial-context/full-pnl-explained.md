# Company P&L: what a Marketing Lead should understand

This is **company-level financial context**, not a statement of personal P&L ownership.

| Line | Baseline (simulated) | Relationship to marketing |
|---|---:|---|
| Completed GMV (memorandum only) | €120,000,000 | Commercial activity, **not revenue** |
| Platform fee revenue | €6,000,000 | Affected by active transactions and take rate |
| Recognized SaaS revenue | €1,800,000 | Affected by paid subscriptions and retention |
| **Total company revenue** | **€7,800,000** | Influenced, not solely controlled by marketing |
| Cost of revenue | (€1,950,000) | Transaction / service-delivery costs |
| **Gross profit** | **€5,850,000** | Revenue after direct delivery costs |
| Marketing | (€2,000,000) | Direct marketing budget accountability |
| Sales | (€1,200,000) | Separate from marketing; includes sales resources |
| Product / Engineering | (€1,100,000) | Product development and operations |
| General & Administrative | (€700,000) | Corporate overhead |
| **Operating profit** | **€850,000** | Full-company operating result |

## P&L is not cash flow

Revenue can be recognized before cash is received. SaaS subscriptions can involve deferred revenue, and platform transactions can involve settlement timing. This simplified model does not provide balance sheet, working capital, taxes or financing.

## Marketing impact example

A hypothetical **additional** €100K pilot generates €180K genuinely incremental platform revenue at a 75% incremental gross margin. It adds €135K gross profit and €100K operating expense, yielding an **illustrative €35K uplift** to operating profit. This is valid only under the specified assumptions; full costs and incrementality must be verified.

## What I would report to a CEO

Spend vs plan; account quality and activation; pipeline status; expected contribution by activity; effect of incremental budget on gross profit and operating profit; uncertainty and next decision. I would not claim all company revenue as marketing-generated.
